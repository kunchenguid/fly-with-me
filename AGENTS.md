# Fly With Me

The engine's rules, for agents and anyone changing `src/`. Most of them were learned by breaking something. The other docs each own one thing: `VISION.md` is what the page is for, the experience it is meant to create, and the test for any change; `CONTRIBUTING.md` holds every command (serve, bundle, checks, bench, capture, publish) and the scenery contract's prose; `docs/perf-notes.md` holds what a frame costs and what is left to win; `README.md` is the front door, with how to use the page and pointers. Do not repeat one file in another.

## Layout

A Three.js page with no assets and no dependencies. `index.html` is the page and the import map; `src/main.js` is the engine, `src/color-grade.js` the approved color grade, `src/noise.js` the CPU noise, and `src/water.js`, `src/milky-way.js` and `src/galaxy-matter.js` the approved water and night sky; `library/` is the scenery (`contract.js`, `index.js`, one file per biome, species, ruin type and prop). The browser loads them as modules from a static server. `tools/bundle.mjs` folds it all into `dist/index.html`, which `.github/workflows/pages.yml` publishes; `dist/` is generated and ignored, never edited. The bundler folds only static `import ... from` and `export const|let|function|class|default`, so keep modules to those forms, with no dynamic import or `import.meta` and top-level await only in the entry, and run the checks on the source page and on the bundle after an engine change. Keep every path relative so the page works under a project subpath. Three.js is pinned to r185.1 in the page's head; an upgrade is deliberate work with a visual diff, because the WebGPU renderer and TSL still rename things between monthly releases. `tools/` and `tests/` never ship. `assets/` holds the README's hero image only, and neither the bundler nor Pages touches it. `media/`, `review.html`, `review-assets/` and `.lavish/` are local review surfaces, gitignored.

## Terrain and climate

- The CPU heightfield is the only terrain truth; the GPU only reads it. Anything that needs a height (bird, camera, props, sound) reads `heightAt` from that window, interpolating the exact rendered triangle, not bilinearly across a quad, or props float on steep terrain.
- Biome color belongs to the actual triangle, not an interpolated colored corner: the indexed main grid reconstructs its triangle samples in the fragment shader (older studies use `fc`). Shore masks use actual fragment height to avoid triangular beach teeth. When `colorNode` reads a color attribute, leave `vertexColors` off.
- One climate rule: `biomeWeights` on the CPU and the terrain `colorNode` on the GPU are the same soft cells, measured from the nearest cell before the exponential so single precision never underflows. Anything that needs a biome reads `biomeAt`; placement reads the tree cell's center, so a tree's species must have weight in its cell's mix, not at its base. The three field seeds are hashed from the seed, not sliced from its bits.
- Placement RNG stays independent from asset-detail RNG. Grass bending reads immutable geometry-attribute height, never an already transformed `positionLocal.y`.

## Sky, light and time

- The day clock (`dayPhase`, `DAY_SECONDS`) and the sun's clock (`solar`) differ: `solar` runs at one pace by day and faster through a night that is `NIGHT_SHARE` of the cycle, blended over the twilights. Palette keys, `skyBodies`, and the sky events' spans and weights are all in solar phase; anything keyed to the sun's height reads `solar(dayPhase)` or the bodies, never the day clock. `INTRO.stretch` is a day-clock rate on top of that pace.
- Sky and fog share `horizonTint`, including its directional warmth and cloud crossing color; day palettes own both, and changing only a fog uniform breaks the boundary. Gentle local air gives way to complete fog before the streamed terrain ends; height fog and soft cloud volumes cover cloud crossings. Above the deck (`uAbove`) the tint is a haze that keeps the day's hue and its sunward warmth, never a flat cloud white, or the sun vanishes into it; only the whiteout goes white. The cloud sea is lit from a normal taken by finite differences of its own heap, on a ramp that follows the sun's height so it stays sculpted at noon, and the far sea fades into `horizonTint` like the sky.
- `uSunDir` is always the true sun, even below the horizon. One directional light is the sun by day and the moon by night; it may change direction only while its intensity is zero, and it starts only once the disc reaches the horizon, or trees throw shadows uphill from a sun below it.
- The approved Milky Way is `src/milky-way.js` plus `src/galaxy-matter.js`: fixed star instancing and generated stellar light, with one branching extinction field shared by both bakes and no catalog growth during flight. The flight's night bearing comes from that same field, never a second copy. Keep `paintedClouds` shared by dome and stars; neither may draw over opaque clouds, and daylight and opaque deck crossings skip the star draw. No look switch ships. After sky changes run `tests/night-checks.js` on both backends and the bundle, plus `node tests/galaxy-checks.mjs`.

## Water

- `src/water.js` owns the approved jade water: its depth reads the exact CPU terrain triangle, its two ripple scales filter by pixel footprint, its light reads the shared day palette, and all motion follows simulation time. Keep it one opaque draw on the existing grid, with no reflection or depth passes and no new textures. Its pixel contracts are in `tests/flight-checks.js`; when isolating scenery for a capture, assign boolean visibility (`isLight === true`), since Three.js treats `visible = undefined` as visible.

## Scenery

- The library is the contributor surface and the engine is closed to it: entries are data, or `bake`/`build`/`place` functions over kits (tree, masonry, prop) that only produce vertex-colored geometry and positions. `validateLibrary` runs at load and `validateBaked` measures every baked geometry; both must keep refusing colors outside the swatch envelope (judged in sRGB; a neutral near-white is a tint carrier), unknown ids and budgets, by entry name. A new capability for contributors is a new kit method or a new entry field with a validator rule, never a hole in the engine. `CONTRIBUTING.md` is the contract's prose; keep it and `library/contract.js` saying the same thing.
- Every species is one baked tree in three pools (wood, crown, distant crown) sharing the crown morph; the distant crown keeps every `keepEvery`-th card enlarged by `distantScale`, both derived from the card count. Tree and site records carry `top` and `radius` for the flight, whose `terrainAhead` follows the arc of the current turn rate with a corridor `radius + 18` wide; the low-pass check measures against that same look-ahead. Keep tall species inside what an 11 m/s climb can clear, or the clearance clamp hops the bird.
- Ruin sites live on `SITE_CELL` cells, deterministic per cell, thinned by `SITE_ODDS`, the biome's `ruins` welcome and the fit of the ground; `sunward` types face the sunrise azimuth taken from `SKY_EVENTS`. Props place per tree cell from their own random stream. Site records, and prop records for kinds that declare `obstacle`, are obstacles for the bird and the camera like trees.
- Detail levels never dither: a screen-space dissolve between two crowns of different shape reads as a stripe of grain. The full crown morphs into the distant one over `CROWN_FADE` (kept cards grow to the species' `distantScale`, the rest shrink) using per-card `card` and per-instance `base`/`spin` attributes, so the distant crown must stay exactly the kept cards enlarged. Trees shrink into `base` at the ring edge because the fog is thin there. WebGPU allows eight vertex buffers per material, which the full crown is close to. The crown assignment margins in `placeTrees` must exceed one ring rebuild step plus the camera's maximum orbit distance, or a rebuild pops.
- The ground shade sheet is painted in world order and must upload with `flipY` off, or every shade mirrors in z and jumps when the sheet re-centers. The shadow frame follows the bird in whole shadow-map texels across the light's plane.

## Flight, steering and camera

- Steering is both ways and outranks the plan: sideways it turns the bird; up and down it moves `cam.pitch` by the left button's own rule and sets `state.aim`, a climb angle whose ends are the ends of the bird's own envelope, with `state.aimHold` saying how much of the vertical is the viewer's. While held, the aim replaces the altitude controller's rate, kept off the ground ahead and under a ceiling by the same clearance rule; a low pass under way is dropped, and at the hand-back the cloud schedule is re-based on where the bird was left. Heading stays wrapped, since the resume path wraps it too.
- The camera has no director and no spring: it is placed rigidly on the bird every frame, so the bird is the orbit pivot and any smoothing of its position reads as lag and pull. The viewer's orbit persists; never add auto-recentering. Right-drag moves orbit yaw into the bird's heading while the camera holds still in the world; its vertical is the left button's camera pitch and the bird's aim at once, so the bird flies where the viewer looks, and either button's framing is saved on release. Left, right and wheel follow World of Warcraft.
- Sky events steer the bird: `SKY_EVENTS` finds the sun's and moon's horizon crossings on the same arcs `skyBodies` draws, and `updateSunward` blends a capped turn toward the low body into the yaw target while silencing the wander. Nightfall adds one turn of the same kind: `isNight` is the sunset-to-sunrise span of those crossings, and `updateNightward` arms at its onset, waits for the sunset pull to let go, turns once onto `galaxy.brightest`, the core `brightestMatter` finds in the sky's own field, and is done for that night. Only one pull applies at a time, the event first; steering releases both. Anything that changes the sky arcs or the flight path changes where the six-minute check flies, so its terrain contracts must stay path-independent.
- The opening (`INTRO`, `intro`, `updateIntro`) plays for every world that is not resumed, from `state.t = 0`: it overrides only the yaw target and the cloud schedule's `wantHigh`, and stretches the day clock (`dayRate`) while the bird climbs and holds above the deck so the sun stays low enough for the default framing. `releaseSunward` ends it, and ending sets `cloudOrigin = state.t`, the origin the schedule counts its 300 s cycle from (persisted in the resume record), so a full low stretch follows. A fresh world's `dayPhase`, start position and heading are chosen for it: `SUNRISE_AZIMUTH` is one constant shared with the sunward ruins, and the start scores the opening path over the candidates. The title card (`TITLE`, `title`, `updateTitle`) starts three seconds into the turn toward the sun only while the opening is still playing, then finishes regardless, driven from `state.t` in `advance`, never by CSS transitions, so a pause holds it and the checks can step it. Tests read `intro`, `opening`, `title`, `titleCard`, `cloudCycle`, `dayRate` and `capture`, which renders the scene into a small float target and returns rows top first on both backends; dispose waits for it.

## Page, memory and rendering

- The page opens on a white veil (`#loading`) and lifts it only from `unveil`, after the first frame's GPU work is done (the queue on WebGPU, a fence on WebGL2) and a browser frame has passed; `ready` and the enabled Begin flip together there, and `openWorld` in the checks waits for `ready`, so nothing may show the world or enable Begin earlier. The gate (`#begin`) carries only the button and a hint, with no render loop behind it; the failure hook in the page reuses the veil.
- Memory lives in two localStorage keys, settings and resume, read once at load through `remember` and validated field by field; a URL seed always beats the remembered world. Flight saves are throttled by wall clock and flushed on pause, hide, leave and dispose, never before Begin. The browser checks therefore clear storage and run inside a fresh frame; a check that reloads the top page would lose its own script.
- Work is bounded: whole-tree instancing per species, reduced distant crowns, a local grass window, small pools for boulders and ruin sites, and fixed-size terrain storage, so world geometry never accumulates. Rendering is capped at two million pixels and 1.5 device pixels per CSS pixel. Only the scene pass is multisampled, the sky dome draws last among the opaque objects, and everything past tone mapping stays in eight bits; `docs/perf-notes.md` says why. Shader time follows the simulation clock. Even one-shot redraws go through `setAnimationLoop`; direct calls reuse cached TSL FRAME passes. Pause and visibility handlers stop rendering and suspend audio; audio is Begin-gated; teardown must finish pending GPU readbacks before destroying their buffers, then release graphics resources and close audio.
- Cost is measured, not guessed: `tools/bench.js` and `tools/parity.js` (commands in `CONTRIBUTING.md`) show what a change costs and whether the picture moved, and `docs/perf-notes.md` records what they found. Read it before optimizing anything and add to it after. One trap from it belongs here: do not trim the shadow map's casters by distance from the bird. The frame is a 720 m box across the light but an open tube along it, from the shadow camera's near plane a kilometre up-light to its far plane beyond, so a tree well outside any sphere can still be the caster a low sun throws across the frame, and dawn is the moment that would break.
- `tools/capture-launch-media.mjs` records the launch film and the hero still from the page itself, headless, and ships nothing; `CONTRIBUTING.md` says how to run it and `media/` is ignored. Two things in it are load-bearing. The page's own veil, gate and controls must stay painted while the engine's canvas is being copied: with nothing over it the canvas is composited straight to the screen and each copy costs a whole frame, so the recording leaves those layers out of its composite instead of hiding them. And the recorded sound must be the page's own destination tap: bridging it into a second `AudioContext` runs on a second clock, under-runs, and loses a third of the samples. `assets/hero.png` is updated by hand from what the tool writes.
- Run `tests/flight-checks.js` through a real browser after engine changes, on both backends, the three seeds and the bundle; the commands and the remaining manual checks are in `CONTRIBUTING.md`.

## Approved looks

Each was chosen by eye on a review board and is the reference a change is compared against: a change to any of them shows its before and after at the same seed and vantage, with `tools/parity.js` reading the finished picture. The exploratory studies the choices were made from are not in the tree; they are in history, last at `addee2b`, and no look switch ships.

- **The soft illustrated rendering with Storybook Punch color**, approved at both distances in the Wildsong study: broad diffuse lighting bands without a physical specular lobe, restrained bump, softened shadows, richer daylight blue, elevated saturation and contrast, stronger bloom, ACES, edge-preserving reconstruction and FXAA. `src/color-grade.js` owns the grade; dawn, dusk and night retain their distinct palettes.
- **The jade water** (`src/water.js`): mint shelves fading into deeper turquoise, gentle elongated ripples breaking the sky and sun into soft strokes, a narrow shore wash that takes the day's light and does not glow at night. Record: `tests/water-review.md`.
- **The star-led Milky Way** (`src/milky-way.js`, `src/galaxy-matter.js`): fine stars, branching dust, cool stellar clouds and a brighter ivory-pink core, chosen from several approaches against a photograph that was a reference only and is never loaded. Record: `tests/milky-way-review.md`.
- **The sky through a day**: more palette keys around dawn and dusk than at noon, a sun-side band, a rose belt opposite a low sun, a moon with its own lit face; above the deck the horizon keeps the day's hue and its sunward warmth, and the cloud sea's tops are lit from their own slope, warm sunward, blue in the hollows, blushing opposite a low sun, moonlit at night.
- **Original procedural art throughout**: painted canvas textures, curved branches, canopy-oriented leaf normals, needle, frond and petal cards, grass and feathered wings; every tree one baked trunk, limbs and crown of painted cards; every ruin from one masonry kit, mossy at the foot. Nothing is downloaded.

## Development and testing

Running fly-with-me in a real browser can be resource intensive. Any browser tabs created for development and testing needs to be torn down immediately once it's not longer needed. Do not leave a live browser rendering fly-with-me in the background hanging.
